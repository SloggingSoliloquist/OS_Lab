FCFS is fairly simple so no comment. 
Notes for the other ones: 
SJF, SRTF, Priority scheduling (both types) and RR are very similar. 
ALL of them have this check for processes that have arrived by the current_time: 
while(i<n and processes[i].arrival_time<=current_time)
{
    push to the queue;
    i++;
}
and the check to fastforward if the queue is empty: 
if(queue.empty())
{
    current_time+=process[i].arrival_time; (current_time++ in the case of SRTF)
    break;
}

The non-preemptive ones are straight forward, the while loop will have some sort of condition like
while(i<n || !ready_queue.empty())
and after the two common checks, they'll pop from the queue and update everything about the process. 

The preemptive ones are similar to each other in the sense that, they rely on keeping track of  the
number of processes that have completed.
while(completed<n)

inside, the first two checks are pretty much the exact same. After the first two checks, they pop a process out, 
and then instead of directly updating, they only move forward in time by some fixed amount (one unit for SJF and priority_p, the time quantum
for RR), and then checks if the remaining_time is 0 or not. if 0, then they update the process with all of its stuff like turnaround time, 
waiting time etc., and if not, they push the process back into the queue. 
RR performs an extra check simular to the very first check in this document, after it pops a process and lets it execute.
